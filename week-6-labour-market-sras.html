Check yourself: why is the curve flat on the left?

There's spare capacity and wages are sticky. When prices rise, revenue goes up but wage costs don't, so profit per unit rises and firms hire more and produce more. Menu costs keep some prices sticky too. So extra AD mainly raises output, not prices.

Check yourself: what does this mean for fiscal policy?

Near point A, a fiscal stimulus moves the economy towards potential with little inflation. Near the LRAS, the same stimulus mostly raises prices, so governments shouldn't stimulate during an upswing.

Check yourself: what happens when you make wages and prices more sticky?

The SRAS gets flatter, so each rise in AD produces more output and less inflation. That's the Keynesian case for fiscal policy. More flexibility gives a steeper curve, closer to the neoclassical view.

Check yourself: name three reasons DL slopes down

Diminishing marginal returns to labour, the substitution effect (firms swap expensive labour for capital such as Gen AI) and the output effect (labour is a derived demand, so higher wages mean higher prices, fewer sales and less labour needed).

Check yourself: why does the market SL slope up if an individual's can bend backwards?

An individual may choose more leisure at very high wages, but across the whole market, higher wages pull new workers in.

Check yourself: how could expansionary policy shift SL?

A higher minimum wage can draw more people into work, shifting SL right. Higher welfare payments may discourage low-paid work. The overall effect on SL is hard to predict.
